# Video Retention Research Summary

## Key Findings for Educational Content

### 1. Progressive Reveals (Highest Impact)
**Research:** 2023 study with 133 students found progressive reveals improved retention with medium-to-large effects
- **Don't show everything at once** - reveal elements as narration mentions them
- **Use covers/masks** - white rectangles that disappear on cue
- Creates "what's next?" anticipation

### 2. Motion vs. Static Images
**Best Practice from CapCut/Adobe guidance:**
- Use Ken Burns zoom (10-20%) when: image is simple, viewer needs to focus on detail
- **Keep static when**: image has multiple elements viewers need to study, complex diagrams, lists
- Over 30% zoom = viewer watches camera, not content

### 3. List Reveals Drive Engagement
**From viral video analysis:**
- Reveal items one-by-one as narrated
- Each reveal gets a pop sound
- Creates micro-payoffs that boost completion rate
- Works for: numbered lists, comparisons, multiple examples

### 4. Retention Benchmarks (YouTube)
- First 30 seconds: lose 20-30% of viewers
- Videos 10-20 min: target 35-45% retention
- Videos 20+ min: target 25-35% retention

### 5. Pattern Interrupts
**Optimal frequency:** Every 45-90 seconds
- Music mood changes
- Section whooshes  
- Text reveals
- Don't overdo it - ruins flow

### 6. Visual Hierarchy
**Typito/Fotor research:**
- Poor visual hierarchy = viewer leaves before answer
- Clear focus point = better retention
- Avoid competing elements on screen

## Applied to Your Workflow

### When to Use STATIC (no zoom):
- Images with 3+ labeled items
- Comparison charts
- Lists being revealed progressively
- Complex diagrams that need study time

### When to Use ZOOM:
- Single subject images
- Emotional moments (face close-ups)
- Transitional establishing shots
- Simple scenes with one focus point

### List Reveal Pattern:
```
Image has: [Apple] [Banana] [Mango]
- Start: All three covered with white rectangles
- "I like a lot of fruits" - all still covered
- "apple" - white cover slides off apple + pop sound
- "banana" - white cover slides off banana + pop sound  
- "and mango" - white cover slides off mango + pop sound
```
